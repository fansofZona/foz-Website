The portal makes headlines as chaos — hundreds of players, portals crashing,
offers made in group chats. We spent a winter turning five years of transfer
announcements into one graph, and the chaos has a shape.

The first surprise is silence at the center. Most movement is not the
marquee-all-hands-forward dashboard drama; it is a quiet trickle of players
moving down, not up. Roughly three quarters of tracked transfers went to a
school at or below the prior school's competitive tier.

The second is how concentrated the big money is. The number of programs that
are *net buyers* — importing more than they export — is a short list, and it
barely moved across five years:

We built the dataset so someone could check the takes, not multiply them.
Reading our own graph, three honest notes:

- **The portal is not a transfer market; it's a second draft.** The name
  sounds transactional; the data reads like a scouting cycle that got louder.
- **Power programs skim, they don't poach the whole roster.** The transfer
  volumes at the top are mostly depth plus one star per year.
- **Eligibility flags matter.** Two datasets that count "announced" versus
  "cleared" players tell different stories. Ours carries both.

The cleaned graph is published and free. The takeaway for fans: every winter
you will see the same panic in the replies. The numbers say the market turned
over faster, got noisier to watch — and let most players do the same thing
college athletes have always done, which is change teams and hope for
something a little closer to home.
