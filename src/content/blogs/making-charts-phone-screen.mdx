We spent a season publishing charts and watching most of them die in a group
chat. This week we're writing down what we learned so the next batch starts
one level up. None of it is about fancy animation — four of the five rules are
about losing information on purpose.

**Rule zero: the axis starts where the data starts.** A zero-based axis is for
bar heights, not for a comparison of two lines that never get close to zero.
Every year some analyst chart inflates a two-percentage-point gap into a chasm
by starting the axis at an arbitrary floor. We use D3's `.nice()` and
`.domain()` and then *we look at the chart* before writing a word about it.

**Rule two: label the axis, not the chart.** The best figure in an article
works without the caption. If your `y` axis says "n" and your reader has to
walk back to the sentence above to discover it's *dugouts*, you have made ink
they must trade for attention.

**Rule three: worst case is the sidebar.** Assume the chart will be seen at
640 pixels wide in a dark-mode chat app. Gridlines thin, annotation off, one
takeaway maximum. If it needs three numbers to make sense, split it into
three figures.

**Rule four: interactivity is for exploration, not first reads.** A hover
tooltip is a reward for curiosity, not a delivery mechanism for the finding.
Every chart on this site has both layers: the static version carries the
headline, and hovering reveals the point-level details.

We rebuilt our whole chart pipeline around this earlier this year. The
components that make it possible are the same ones that render this page —
they ship in `src/components/charts`, and the next blog post is going to show
you how to use them in your own MDX.
