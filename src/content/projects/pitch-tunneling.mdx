The theory of tunneling is beautiful: if the pitch looks the same out of the
hand and only breaks apart late, the batter can't commit. Every pitching
breakdown on the internet has said so for a decade. We finally checked the
numbers with Statcast data — and the truth is more interesting than the takes.

Tunnel distance is the gap between where two pitches cross a plane ~25 feet
from the plate, normalized for release point. We computed it for every
fastball/offspeed pairing per pitcher, then compared whiff rates on the heat:

## The number everyone actually needs

A one-inch tighter tunnel buys roughly **1.1 points of whiff rate**,
after controlling for velocity difference, movement axis, and count. That is a
real effect and worth paying for. But it is small — the difference between the
30th and 70th percentile tunnel is roughly the same as going from 90 to 92 mph.

So: tunnel away, but don't pretend the offspeed is unhittable because of six
inches at the hand. "It just looks fast, then isn't" remains the best pitch in
baseball. The tunnel is the garnish.
