Every marathoner hears it from a friend they ran with once: "You absolutely
started too fast." We turned that folklore into an estimate using 40,000
finisher splits. For each of the 5km segments, we look at whether your split
is faster or slower than your overall goal pace.

The pattern is brutal and repeatable. The average amateur's second half is
**nine minutes slower** than the first. And the entire distribution of
outcomes is downstream of that first 10km:

## The number to race by

The cleanest result is the practical one: for every 5 seconds per km a runner
goes out faster than their target even-split pace, they lose **~2 finisher
minutes** beyond it, and the penalty compounds at the 30–35km wall. Even-split
pacing isn't perfect — it's *least bad*, and it beats enthusiasm in 87% of
runs.

If you take one chart from this website to the start line, make it the
spreadsheet version of that line. Ten seconds in the first 10km does not feel
like ten seconds. It feels like two minutes, and you pay for it at mile
nineteen, in cash.
