Our recommendation

Academic Wordmark for the website.

It gives the research a clear editorial voice and matches the restrained V3 layouts. Choose FoZ Monogram if a compact club emblem matters most; choose Field of View for a more abstract research identity.

Field of View

The question comes into focus.

An open field, an observation at its center. Two opposing corners give the mark structure without closing it in.

The boundaries suggest a playing surface and the frame of an investigation. The central dot is the observation: a ball, an event, a question worth studying. The open corners leave room for the next finding.

Best for an identity that can grow across sports, data tools, and publications.

Tradeoff The symbol is abstract; use the full club name when introducing it to a new audience.

FoZ Monogram

Three letters. One shared structure.

The F and Z share a midline. A circular counter cuts through their meeting point to make room for the o.

The diagonal gives the mark direction, while the shared counter keeps the lettering connected. It is compact enough for an avatar and recognizable as FoZ when paired with the club name.

Best for club recognition, profile images, apparel, and small digital placements.

Tradeoff Its athletic energy is stronger than the other directions; keep the surrounding typography quiet.

Academic Wordmark

Let the name do the work.

A serif signature with carefully tightened spacing. The full name leads, with a FoZ imprint for smaller uses.

Geist Serif gives the name the cadence of a publication. The compact FoZ signature carries that same voice into footers and avatars, while Geist Sans keeps functional information clear.

Best for the simple, academic website direction and research report covers.

Tradeoff The letterforms need more room at small sizes; the compact signature starts at 24px.

Give the mark room.

Keep at least one quarter of the compact mark’s width clear on every side. Use the same unit for symbol lockups; use half the capital height for the academic wordmark.

Keep color purposeful.

Use navy for reading and red for links or a single point of emphasis. Reverse the logo to white on navy or red. A mark uses one ink at a time.

Make the work legible.

Left-align text. Keep reading columns around 65 characters. Label charts directly and distinguish series with line styles as well as color.
